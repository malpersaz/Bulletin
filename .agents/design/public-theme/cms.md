# CMS Public Theme Profile

## Use When

Use this profile for company, service, product, institutional, or conversion-oriented public themes.

## Target User

Visitors should understand what the organization offers, why it is credible, and how to move toward contact, service, product, or account actions.

## First Viewport

- Lead with the organization, offer category, or primary service/product promise.
- Show a real product, service, client, reference, or institutional image when available.
- Keep the next section partially discoverable on common public viewport sizes.

## Content Hierarchy

- Prioritize `mod_service`, `mod_product`, `mod_reference`, `mod_client`, `mod_page`, and `mod_content`.
- Use news and publications as proof or updates, not as the dominant visual system unless requested.
- Keep product, service, reference, and client paths ahead of secondary editorial, author, video, or gallery rails on the homepage unless `templates/<theme>/DESIGN.md` documents a deliberate exception.
- Preserve supporting coverage for `mod_news`, `mod_publication`, `mod_video_embed`, and `mod_imagecategories`; make them quieter than the primary business modules instead of removing them.
- Keep search, language switching, login/register, and account actions intact.

## Header And Footer

- Header should make primary navigation and conversion paths easy to scan.
- Footer should support trust: menu branches, contact paths, social/share surfaces, and site metadata.

## Card And Detail Rhythm

- Product and service cards should expose image, title, short summary, and action path consistently.
- Reference and client modules should read as proof, not decorative badges.
- Detail pages should make specifications, main content, media, and related links easy to compare.

## Visual Direction

- Prefer quiet, utilitarian structure over oversized decorative composition.
- Use contrast and spacing to clarify offerings.
- Avoid single-hue palettes that make every module feel identical.

## Motion

- Motion should support feedback and orientation only.
- Avoid delaying access to service, product, or contact paths.

## Mobile Behavior

- Put the primary offer and first useful action early.
- Keep product/service comparison cards compact but legible.
- Preserve touch-friendly account, language, search, and navigation controls.
- Hide empty homepage module shells rather than showing business-themed filler text.
